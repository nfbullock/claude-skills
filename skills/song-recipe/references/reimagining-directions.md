# Reimagining directions — taxonomy of OP-1 cover moves

**Sequencer-friction weighting (2026-05-27, JOURNAL + memory feedback_op1f_sequencer_friction_current_stage):** directions that lean on the step sequencer (B, C, D, J) are DE-WEIGHTED at Nick's current stage — prefer live-play, finger-drumming, paste-and-step-on-tape, and sampling-led directions. Sequencer-led picks need an explicit reason.

## Licensed-by index — directions are derived, not picked

The recipe's typed essence list (see the song-recipe SKILL.md breakdown) comes FIRST. Directions are **derived from what the essence tags say is kept**, not picked from the menu below — the lettered catalog is the device-native realization vocabulary, the OP-1-flavored way to execute whatever the derivation calls for. Each row carries **one coherence constraint**: the thing that, if violated, breaks the cover *on its own terms* — never fidelity to the original (the 40oz doctrine holds; no direction is measured against the source recording).

- **Melodic Hook kept** → harmonic re-coloring under the kept melody, or groove subversion beneath it. *Constraint: vocal-range fit — the phrasing must survive the new key/tempo within real breath and sustain limits. The contour itself doesn't change, though its syncopation may shift to lock with the new groove.*
- **Harmonic Loop kept** → contrafact (new melody/words over the kept changes) or timbral translation (same voicings, radically different engine). *Constraint: harmonic gravity — the new top-line must honor the loop's tension/release; climaxes resolve on chord tones.*
- **Groove/Riddim kept** → dub subtraction (strip the foreground, expose the riddim) or rhythmic augmentation over the kept kick/snare relationship. *Constraint: micro-timing preservation — BPM may drift slightly, but the swing/pocket character IS the essence; lose it and the riddim is gone.*
- **Lyric/Prosodic Narrative kept** → spoken-word repositioning (words as rhythm, melody removed) or affective inversion (the words over an opposing-mood setting). *Constraint: the new setting must honor natural speech stress and the rhyme scheme's stability character, or the prosody seams show.*
- **Timbral Signature kept** → fragment flip — isolate the sound object, sample it, let it seed new patterns. EP/OP-1-native, and exactly what the sampling-led de-weighting above already favors. *Constraint: structural freedom — the original macro-form is discarded; the timbre seeds a NEW form.*
- **Structural Gesture kept** *(house rule — our own synthesis; the research left this row unmapped)* → instrumentation, genre, and groove are all free, but the gesture must survive at the same formal position (the dropout still detonates before the final chorus). Mark the gesture's bar position in the lesson's arrangement table.

Note the lettered menu under-covers the Groove/Riddim and Timbral Signature rows (dub subtraction and fragment flip have no lettered entry yet) — when the essence tags point there, realize the direction straight from the index row with OP-1 sampling and tape gestures. A derived direction doesn't need a letter.

The catalog of distinct ways to translate a song onto the OP-1 Field. Each direction is a *flavor* — a stance on what to keep, what to swap, and which OP-1 features carry the cover. Per recipe, derive **2–3** from the typed essence list via the index above, present them as a menu in the chat. Then pick one and commit.

Each entry below has:
- **The move** — the one-line stance.
- **When it fits** — what kind of source material it works on.
- **OP-1 ingredients** — the engines / FX / tape style / sequencer that make it go.
- **What the listener hears** — the resulting vibe.
- **Lift** — light / medium / heavy (how much new technique it requires from Nick).

## A. Lofi-tape-warble

*[licensed by: Melodic Hook, Harmonic Loop — timbral translation of the kept material]*

- **The move:** keep the song mostly intact, but bake in tape character — saturation, hiss, continuous wow.
- **When it fits:** acoustic / folk / singer-songwriter songs whose essence is the chord motion and vocal melody, not the production. Anything Damien Rice, Chris Smither, Jack Johnson, Nick Drake-flavored.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** *vintage* or *porta* tape style (`shift + tape`); cluster or string engine for the chord pad; continuous gray (95–105%) during the chord recording for the wow; spring FX on the chord track; light terminal on master for hiss.
- **What the listener hears:** the original, *aged* — sounds like it was found on a cassette in a shoebox.
- **Lift:** light. One new technique max (the continuous-gray wow move).

## B. Sequencer-flip

*[licensed by: Harmonic Loop — timbral/groove translation of the kept changes]*

- **The move:** replace strummed/picked parts with a sequenced version — pattern, endless, or finger sequencer drives a pulse instead of a hand-played rhythm.
- **When it fits:** songs whose hook is the chord progression, not the strumming pattern. Folk, pop, country covers benefit hugely. Punk is great for this too — sequence the riff.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** pattern sequencer on a synth engine (dimension or fm or pulse for plucky); drum sampler for the kit; cluster or dr wave engine for a layered pad behind. Often: bass on endless sequencer.
- **What the listener hears:** the song's bones with mechanical pulse — feels less "human-played," more "lofi beat tape."
- **Lift:** medium. Introduces a sequencer if Nick hasn't used one yet.

## C. Half-time invert

*[licensed by: Melodic Hook, Harmonic Loop — groove subversion beneath the kept melody/changes]*

- **The move:** keep the chords and melody, slow the *feel* by putting the snare on 3 instead of 2 and 4. Optionally also pitch down via tape speed.
- **When it fits:** any song that's currently at a medium-fast backbeat. Especially good on punk, ska, college rock — the contrast is dramatic.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** drum sampler with snare programmed to 3 only; pattern sequencer at the original BPM; optional tape speed at `shift + gray` 50% during chord overdubs.
- **What the listener hears:** swampy, syrupy, suddenly-emotional version. "Wonderwall but at midnight."
- **Lift:** medium. The halftime drum pattern is the new compositional move; if Nick hasn't done one yet, it teaches it explicitly.

## D. Double-time / chipmunk lift

*[licensed by: Melodic Hook, Harmonic Loop — groove subversion beneath the kept melody/changes]*

- **The move:** the inverse — speed up the surface, with optional pitch-shifting via tape speed for chipmunk effect.
- **When it fits:** slow ballads and dirges that reveal a hidden bounce when pushed. Also great as a chorus-only move within an otherwise normal cover (verse normal, chorus chipmunked).
- **OP-1 ingredients:** record at slow tempo, play back at `shift + gray` 200%; or sequence the part at double time on pattern sequencer.
- **What the listener hears:** dreamy, slightly-too-fast, vintage-sample-flip energy. The "Avalanches treatment."
- **Lift:** medium-heavy. Tape speed manipulation is a new technique.

## E. Strip-to-bones

*[licensed by: Melodic Hook — the kept melody, exposed]*

- **The move:** reduce the song to the absolute minimum — one chord pad, one vocal-line synth, one drum element. Maybe two of the three.
- **When it fits:** songs whose essence is the melody itself, where the original arrangement obscures it. Great for trying to *hear* the song clearly.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** one engine for the chord pad (cluster), one engine for the melody (string or dr wave), maybe one drum kick on quarter notes. Two tape tracks total.
- **What the listener hears:** the song as a sketch — emotionally exposed, no decoration.
- **Lift:** light. The discipline is harder than the technique.

## F. Digitalize

*[licensed by: Melodic Hook, Harmonic Loop — total timbral translation]*

- **The move:** translate an acoustic/folk/rock song entirely into OP-1 synth voices — cluster pads, fm leads, dr wave organs, sequenced bass. Nothing tries to sound "real."
- **When it fits:** songs whose melody and chord motion are strong enough to survive a total timbre swap. Good for the Shakey Graves / Cage the Elephant / Bowie / Bargain Music kind of source.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** chord pad on cluster (waves 4, spread 70); lead on fm or dr wave; bass on dimension or pulse; drums on dbox (per-key synth kit).
- **What the listener hears:** the song reborn in chiptune / lofi-electronic costume. The point is the *uncanny familiarity* of hearing a known melody in a totally different timbre.
- **Lift:** medium-heavy. Multiple engine choices required; if any of them are new to Nick, only introduce one.

## G. De-electronicize

*[licensed by: Melodic Hook, Harmonic Loop — timbral translation, inverse of F]*

- **The move:** the inverse — take an electronic / synth-pop / EDM song and render it through tape character, keeping it warm and human-feeling.
- **When it fits:** synth-driven originals (Daft Punk, LCD Soundsystem, Depeche Mode, even Bowie's electronic-period stuff). The cover *softens* the original.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** porta tape style; cluster or string engine where the original had synths; spring or mother reverb; continuous gray for warble; drum sampler with softer kit.
- **What the listener hears:** electronic song through a "warm" filter. Same notes, different epoch.
- **Lift:** medium.

## H. Granular-mangle

*[licensed by: Lyric-Prosodic Narrative, Structural Gesture — the kept structure and mood, re-textured]*

- **The move:** keep the song structure but mangle texture — phone FX, terminal bit-crush, chop tape trick — to make it feel broken/glitchy.
- **When it fits:** songs whose lyrics or mood are dark, anxious, or surreal. Bright Eyes, NIN-adjacent, "First Day of My Life" inverted.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** phone FX with high feedback for stutter; terminal at low kHz/bits; tape trick `[6]` chop for builds; cluster or dnasynth for unstable pads.
- **What the listener hears:** familiar song, broken through static. Lofi-as-distress.
- **Lift:** medium-heavy. Multiple FX in play; introduce one new FX at most.

## I. Chord-pad reverb wash

*[licensed by: Harmonic Loop — the kept progression as meditation]*

- **The move:** replace all rhythm/strum elements with a long-sustained chord pad bathed in reverb. The melody floats on top, unhurried.
- **When it fits:** songs whose chord progression is a meditation rather than a propulsion. Pink Floyd, ambient covers, Mazzy Star, slow Neil Young.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** cluster engine (waves 4–6, spread 80, unitor 60); spring or mother FX with high mix; one melody track on string or dr wave; minimal or no drums.
- **What the listener hears:** the song dissolved into its own atmosphere. Time stretches.
- **Lift:** light.

## J. Drum-machine flip

*[licensed by: Melodic Hook, Harmonic Loop — groove subversion; only when Groove/Riddim is NOT the kept essence]*

- **The move:** replace live drums entirely with a drum-machine pattern that recontextualizes the rhythm — boom-bap, trap, dub, motorik.
- **When it fits:** songs whose rhythmic feel is *not* essence (rock, folk, country can all be flipped). Less interesting on songs where the rhythm is the hook (funk, ska — there the rhythm IS the song).
- **OP-1 ingredients:** drum sampler with a custom kit (or dbox kit); pattern sequencer programmed to the new style; original chord progression on tape over the new drums.
- **What the listener hears:** the song wearing a different rhythm's clothes. Best when the new rhythm is *contrastingly* simple to the original.
- **Lift:** medium.

## K. Reverse-swell intro

*[licensed by: Structural Gesture — add-on flourish, compatible with any primary direction]*

- **The move:** open the cover with a reversed take of a chord pad — sucks the listener in, then the song proper begins. Used as the *intro section* of a longer recipe, not as the whole direction.
- **When it fits:** anywhere. It's a flavor add-on, not a standalone direction.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** record a chord pad to T1; lift; drop on T4; resample with tape trick `[5]` reverse held during Out-to-In; place at bar 1.
- **What the listener hears:** an inhale before the song. Cinematic.
- **Lift:** medium. Combines lift/drop, reverse, and Out-to-In resample.

## L. Out-to-In re-tape

*[licensed by: any primary — mastering-style finishing flourish, pairs naturally with Timbral Signature-led recipes]*

- **The move:** record the song, then resample the entire master back through tape onto a free track — bakes tape character twice. End result is the master mix as a single tape file with extra warmth/saturation.
- **When it fits:** as the *final move* on any recipe — a mastering-style flourish.
- **OP-1 ingredients:** `shift + mic` → Out-to-In; record master to a free track at the end of the build.
- **What the listener hears:** the cover *aged*. Most useful when the chosen tape style is *vintage* or *porta*.
- **Lift:** light, but only if Nick has used Out-to-In before.

## Deriving the menu per recipe

**Derivation first.** Read the typed essence list, find what's kept, and let the Licensed-by index name the direction families. Realize each family with the lettered vocabulary above (or straight from the index row where the menu has no letter). Every menu entry should be able to say which essence item licenses it.

**Every direction is an interpretation.** Don't think of one as the "safe" cover and others as the "interpretive" ones — all directions are the rig's version, none is a default-fidelity baseline (see `feedback_covers_not_same_style`). Vary the menu by *kind* of move (tape character, sequencer, rhythmic invert, harmonic move) rather than by how-far-from-original.

### Tiebreakers — applied AFTER the typed-essence derivation, never instead of it

When the derivation licenses more candidates than a menu of 2–3 can hold:

- **Always include one easy direction** (lift = light) so Nick has a confidence path. Usually A, E, or I.
- **Don't pick two from the same family.** A and L are both tape-character moves — only offer one. C and D are inverses — pick one. F and G are inverses — pick one.
- **Match the song's mood, then optionally invert.** A sad acoustic song fits A and I naturally; offering H (broken/glitchy) inverts the mood and is interesting precisely because of the contrast.
- **Optionally include one wildcard** — a direction with a heavier lift or weirder result, when the song supports it. H is the usual wildcard.

If Nick is in **Phase 1** (PB&J), present the menu of 2–3 in the chat, then pick one for him with a one-sentence why. Do not make him choose. If he's in **Phase 2** (Menu of Three), present the three and let him pick.
